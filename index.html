How to Play Chess

Objective

Checkmate your opponent's king — trap it so it is under attack and has no safe square, piece to block with, or way to capture the attacker. There is no 'capturing' the king itself.

The board & setup

An 8×8 grid. Each side starts with 16 pieces on the two rows nearest them: a row of 8 pawns, and behind them, from the outside in, a Rook, Knight, Bishop, Queen, King, Bishop, Knight, Rook. Each queen starts on a square matching her own color. White moves first, then players alternate turns.

How each piece moves

Pawn — moves straight forward one square (two squares on its very first move), but captures one square diagonally forward. It cannot capture straight ahead. Reaching the far end of the board promotes it — in this app it automatically becomes a Queen.

Knight — moves in an L-shape: two squares in one direction, then one square perpendicular. It is the only piece that can jump over others.

Bishop — moves any number of squares diagonally. Each bishop stays on one color for the whole game.

Rook — moves any number of squares horizontally or vertically.

Queen — combines the rook and bishop: any number of squares in any straight or diagonal direction. The most powerful piece.

King — moves exactly one square in any direction. It can never move into check (a square an enemy piece attacks).

Special moves

Castling — once per game, a king and a rook on the same side can move together: the king slides two squares toward the rook, and the rook jumps to the square beside it. Allowed only if neither piece has moved yet, the squares between them are empty, and the king is not in check, does not pass through check, and does not land in check.

En passant — if an enemy pawn moves two squares forward and lands beside your pawn, you may capture it immediately as if it had only moved one square. This chance is only available on the very next move.

Promotion — a pawn reaching the last row becomes a stronger piece (normally your choice; this app always promotes to a Queen).

Check, checkmate & draws

Check — the king is under attack. That player must immediately move out of check.

Checkmate — the king is in check with no legal way out. The game ends; that player loses.

Stalemate — the player to move is not in check but has no legal move at all. The game is a draw.

Playing on this board

Tap a piece to select it — its legal moves light up with a dot (or a ring around a piece you can capture). Tap a highlighted square to move there. Undo takes back the last move, New Game resets the board.
